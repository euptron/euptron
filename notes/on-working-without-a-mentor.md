No mentor means no shortcuts, but it also means no one telling you a problem is "supposed" to be hard.

I've solved things badly, slowly, and alone, and gotten there anyway.

## What the absence teaches you

The absence of a guide forces a different kind of resourcefulness.

You learn to:

1. Read documentation like a **primary source**, not a reference
2. Trust the process of being stuck for a while before a solution shows up
3. Treat forums and issue threads as classrooms

It's slower.

> It's also mine.
